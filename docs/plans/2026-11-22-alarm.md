# Plan · 2026-11-22 · Alarm banner: move the response button out of the disclosure

Branch: `ux/2026-11-21-cycle`  
Backlog item: **rank 4** in `docs/ux-backlog.md`. The alarm banner nests action buttons inside `summary`/`details` controls, so the name, role, and keyboard activation of P1 alarm actions are ambiguous (WCAG 4.1.2, 2.1.1, 1.3.1). Severity 3, effort M.

## Why this one

- **Ranks 1 and 3 are done.** Rank 2 (floor keyboard model) is effort L. It needs a keyboard model across halls, pods, racks, feeds, and pipes in a 1,900-line view, and it doesn't fit one ~300-line cycle.
- **Rank 4 beats rank 5 on reach.** Both are severity 3 and effort M. But the alarm banner is on every signed-in screen, and its primary button is the operator's first response to a P1. The KVM console (rank 5) is only on Sessions.
- **The whole item fits; no slice is needed.** Reading the code:
  - All the banner markup is in one function, `alarmBanner()` in `src/ui/alarms.ts` (lines 157–175).
  - The click handlers in `src/ui/render.ts` find their target with `closest("[data-act]")`. Both `alarm-focus` (on the `summary`) and `alarm-go` (on the button) keep working when the button becomes a sibling of the `details`. So `render.ts` doesn't need to change.
  - The estimate is about 25 lines in `alarms.ts`, about 25 in `styles.css`, 2 in `demo.ts`, and about 70 in specs, for roughly 120–150 lines in total.

## Problem (today, from the code)

`alarmBanner()` in `src/ui/alarms.ts` renders:

```html
<details class="alarm-banner">
  <summary class="alarm-chip" aria-label="Abnormal conditions">
    <span class="pri-mark pri-1" aria-hidden="true">1</span><span>P1 · 7</span>
  </summary>
  <div class="alarm-list" role="list">
    <details class="alarm-row pri-1 unacked">
      <summary data-act="alarm-focus" data-alarm="watch-tray-05">
        <span class="pri-mark pri-1" aria-hidden="true">1</span><b>GPU Tray 05</b>
        <span class="alarm-line">Automatic login on … read: …</span>
        <button type="button" class="act primary" data-act="alarm-go" data-alarm="watch-tray-05">Open serial</button>
      </summary>
      <div class="alarm-extra">
        <button … data-act="alarm-ack">Ack</button>
        <details class="explain-fold"><summary>Explain</summary>…</details>
      </div>
    </details>
    …
  </div>
</details>
```

The problems:

1. **There is a button inside a button.** `<summary>` has an implicit `button` role, and the children of a button are presentational. So the response button ("Open serial", "Open control", "MPU2 KVM", and the others) is nested interactive content (4.1.2):
   - Screen readers may flatten it into the summary's name, which becomes "GPU Tray 05 Automatic login on … Open serial".
   - Some screen readers don't expose it as a separate control at all.
2. **Activation is ambiguous.** The response button is inside the control that toggles the row. Only the `alarm-go` branch in `render.ts` (`event.preventDefault(); event.stopPropagation();`, about line 539) stops a click on it from also toggling the row. Keyboard and assistive-tech activation paths that don't send a plain click to that button toggle the row instead (2.1.1). That is the "operators may expand or collapse the banner instead of responding" impact in the backlog.
3. **The response buttons have no context in their names.** Each row has a button with the same visible name, for example several "Open serial" or "Open inlet". A screen-reader user who lists buttons can't tell which asset each one is for.
4. **The list structure is broken.** `.alarm-list` has `role="list"`, but its children are plain `<details>` elements with no `listitem` role (1.3.1).
5. **Priority isn't conveyed to assistive tech.** The priority number in `.pri-mark` is `aria-hidden="true"` in every row, and no other text says it (1.3.1).
6. **The chip's name hides what it shows.** The chip's `aria-label="Abnormal conditions"` replaces its visible text "P1 · 7". The accessible name doesn't include the visible label (2.5.3), and screen-reader users don't hear the top priority or the count.

Other code that depends on this markup:

- `src/domain/demo.ts` (lines 482–483): the fix-demo beat targets and opens `details.alarm-row:has(summary[data-alarm="watch-…"])`.
- `e2e/alarm.spec.js`: expects `[data-act="alarm-go"]` inside the row `summary`, and uses `.alarm-row[open]`.
- `e2e/fix-demo.spec.js` (lines 87 and 109): `details.alarm-row:has(summary[data-alarm="watch-tray-05"]) [data-act="alarm-go"]`.
- `e2e/fleet.spec.js` (line 24): parses the count from the chip's `innerText` with `/·\s*(\d+)/`.
- `e2e/access.spec.js` (line 132): `.alarm-banner` contains "DI-1 leak rope asserted".
- `styles.css` (lines 984–1037): `.alarm-row > summary` and `.alarm-row button.act`. The mobile rules at lines 3135–3141 and the floor `pointer-events` rule at lines 3269–3270 are unaffected.

## User story

As a keyboard or screen-reader operator who has a P1 alarm, I want each alarm row's response ("Open serial · GPU Tray 05") to be its own named button, separate from the control that expands the row. That way I can respond to the alarm without accidentally expanding or collapsing it. I also want to hear the alarm's priority and the banner's top priority and count.

## Change (for the builder)

1. **Restructure each row in `alarmBanner()`** (`src/ui/alarms.ts`) as:

   ```html
   <div class="alarm-row pri-N[ unacked]" role="listitem">
     <details class="alarm-fold">
       <summary data-act="alarm-focus" data-alarm="ID">
         <span class="pri-mark pri-N" aria-hidden="true">N</span><span class="sr-only">Priority N. </span>
         <b>ASSET</b><span class="alarm-line">TEXT</span>
       </summary>
       <div class="alarm-extra">…Ack + Explain, unchanged…</div>
     </details>
     <button type="button" class="act primary" data-act="alarm-go" data-alarm="ID"
             aria-label="RESPONSE · ASSET">RESPONSE</button>   <!-- only when alarm.response -->
   </div>
   ```

   - Keep the `alarm-row`, `pri-N`, and `unacked` classes on the wrapper, so the existing colour and inset-bar rules still apply.
   - The `aria-label` starts with the visible text (2.5.3). Pass both parts through `esc()`.
   - Nothing interactive is left inside any `summary`, except the existing nested `explain-fold` summary, which contains only text.
2. **Fix the chip name.** Remove `aria-label="Abnormal conditions"` from `.alarm-chip`. Put `<span class="sr-only">Abnormal conditions: </span>` before the visible `P{n} · {count}` text. The accessible name becomes "Abnormal conditions: P1 · 7".
3. **Add one `.sr-only` utility to `styles.css`.** None exists today. Use the standard pattern: `position:absolute; width:1px; height:1px; margin:-1px; padding:0; overflow:hidden; clip:rect(0 0 0 0); white-space:nowrap; border:0`.
4. **Update the row CSS in `styles.css`.**
   - `.alarm-row` becomes `display:flex; align-items:flex-start; gap:8px;`.
   - `.alarm-fold` gets `flex:1 1 auto; min-width:0;`.
   - Move the `.alarm-row > summary` and `::-webkit-details-marker` rules to `.alarm-fold > summary`.
   - Give the response button `flex:none` plus a top margin that lines it up with the summary row's 6px padding.
   - `.alarm-row button.act` and `.alarm-row button.act.primary` already match the button as a descendant. Keep them.
   - No other visual change. The row should look the same as today: the button stays on the right of the first line.
5. **Update the demo selectors** in `src/domain/demo.ts` (lines 482–483):
   - `target`: `'.alarm-row:has(summary[data-alarm="watch-' + FIX_DEVICE + '"]), .alarm-banner'`.
   - `open`: `'.alarm-banner, details.alarm-fold:has(summary[data-alarm="watch-' + FIX_DEVICE + '"])'`. `placeDemoHighlights` only opens `HTMLDetailsElement` nodes, so `open` has to point at the inner `details`.
6. **Leave `src/ui/render.ts` unchanged.** The `alarm-focus`, `alarm-ack`, and `alarm-go` branches keep their current behaviour. The `preventDefault` and `stopPropagation` in `alarm-go` are now redundant but harmless. Leave them, so the diff stays in scope.
7. **Update the specs to the new structure** (see Files to touch), and add the keyboard and naming checks below.

Loading feedback: `goAlarm()` is synchronous: it calls `go()`, opens a floor panel, or starts a local session. No action here takes over 300 ms, so no busy state is added.

## Acceptance criteria

Every criterion is checkable in Playwright. Setup, the same as the existing `e2e/alarm.spec.js`: use a 1440×900 viewport, go to `/#/floor/hall`, sign in as `dsvs.operator`, and wait until the chip reads `P1 · N`.

1. **No button inside a summary.** With the banner open, `.alarm-list summary button` and `.alarm-list summary [data-act="alarm-go"]` both have count 0.
2. **Each response is its own named button.** For every `.alarm-row` with a `[data-act="alarm-go"]` child:
   - The button is a direct child of `.alarm-row`, not a descendant of `details`.
   - Its accessible name is `<visible text> · <asset>`, where the asset is the row's `summary b` text. Check with `toHaveAccessibleName`.
   - Its accessible name starts with its visible text.
   - With the banner open, `page.getByRole("button", { name: "<response> · <asset>" })` resolves to exactly one element for the first row.
3. **The list structure is valid.** `.alarm-list > *` all have `role="listitem"`. The number of `.alarm-list > [role="listitem"]` elements equals N from the chip.
4. **Priority is announced.** The first row's `summary` has an accessible name that matches `/^Priority 1\. /`. Every row's summary name starts with `Priority <its pri-N>.`.
5. **The chip's name includes what it shows.** `.alarm-chip` has an accessible name that matches `/^Abnormal conditions: P1 · \d+$/`, after whitespace normalization. It has no `aria-label` attribute.
6. **The keyboard order is clear.** Focus the chip and press Enter. `.alarm-list` becomes visible.
   - Press Tab once: the first row's `summary` is focused.
   - Press Tab again: that row's response button is focused, not the row's Ack, which is hidden while the row is collapsed.
   - Press Tab again: the second row's `summary` is focused.
7. **Enter or Space on the summary expands the row and doesn't respond to the alarm.** With the first row's summary focused, press Enter.
   - The row's `details.alarm-fold` has `open`.
   - Ack (`[data-act="alarm-ack"]`) is visible.
   - The view is unchanged (the `h1` text is the same as before).
   - Press Space: the row collapses again.
8. **Enter on the response button responds to the alarm and doesn't toggle the row.**
   - Record the first row's `details.alarm-fold` `open` state (false), focus its response button, and press Enter.
   - The app navigates to that alarm's target. For the first floor-hall alarm today, this is Sessions: the `h1` reads `Operate · Sessions`, as in the existing test.
   - The alarm is acked: its row loses `unacked` when the banner is next rendered.
   - Repeat from a fresh sign-in with Space instead of Enter. The result is the same.
9. **Focus is visible.** For the chip, the first row's summary, and its response button, when each is focused by keyboard, its computed `outline-style` is not `none`. The global `:focus-visible` rule at `styles.css` line 131 should cover this, so check it without adding CSS.
10. **The pointer behaviour is unchanged.** The existing test "alarm chip focuses the rack and the action leaves the floor" still passes, with only its selectors updated:
    - Clicking the row's asset (`summary b`) still pans the floor and pulses one `.rackbox.is-pulse`.
    - The row's `details.alarm-fold` is `open`, and Ack is visible.
    - Clicking the response button goes to Sessions.
11. **No layout regression.** With the banner and the first row open at 1440×900, the response button's bounding box is on the same line as the summary: its top is within 8 px of the summary's top, and it is right of the `.alarm-line`. The button is still visible at a 390×844 viewport.
12. **The demo still works.** `e2e/fix-demo.spec.js` passes with its two `alarm-go` selectors updated to `.alarm-row:has(summary[data-alarm="watch-tray-05"]) [data-act="alarm-go"]`. The fix-demo beat at `demo.ts:481` still highlights the tray-05 row and opens it.
13. **The suite is green.** `npm test` in `prototypes/access-control` (vite build, then Playwright) passes with no skipped or removed tests. `fleet.spec.js` and `access.spec.js` pass unchanged. The total diff is under about 300 lines.
